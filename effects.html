Component 02 / Audio processing

Effects processor

Four independent DSP effects and controllable sends, applied to each stereo audio frame as the synthesizer runs.

Component owner: Aryan Moparti

Effects implemented

The processor supports dry passthrough, individual wet buses and a combined output. Each effect retains its state between audio frames.

Effect 01

Chorus

Two delayed copies of the input move slowly in time, using sinusoidally varied delay lengths near 22 milliseconds. Different phase offsets in the stereo channels produce a wider sound.

Effect 02

Reverberation

Four feedback comb filters simulate repeated reflections. Two all-pass filters diffuse the reflections to create a reverb tail distinct from a single echo.

Effect 03

Flanging

A short modulated delay is mixed with the current signal and fed back into the delay. The shifting delay creates moving cancellations and resonances.

Effect 04

Ring modulation

The incoming sample is multiplied by a sinusoidal carrier at 74 Hz. The resulting frequency sidebands give the output a metallic character.

Routing and sample streaming

All effects are parallel so each note can specify a different amount of each effect.

Per-note routing

Each instrument generates a stereo frame. The frame is multiplied by five independently set levels: dry, chorus, reverb, flanger and ring. The values are accumulated into five stereo buses.

Settings on a <note> override defaults from its enclosing <instrument>.

Bounded memory

CSynthesizer::Generate() invokes CEffects::Process() once for each audio frame. The effects use compact circular-delay and feedback buffers rather than loading the entire final selection into RAM.

A 2.5-second processing tail lets delayed sound decay after the last instrument note.

<instrument instrument="ScratchInstrument" dry="0.7" reverb="0.3">
  <note measure="1" beat="1" duration="2"
        sample="are-you-ready.wav" method="baby"
        chorus="0.5" flanger="0.25" />
</instrument>

How the effects component meets the rubric

Our effects component processes audio through four independent effects: chorus, reverberation, flanging, and ring modulation. Each note can specify individual effect-send levels through XML, allowing different combinations throughout the composition. The system processes one stereo sample frame at a time to maintain the required streaming architecture.

What happens to one audio frame

This is how the effects stay compatible with the project's streaming requirement.

1. Route note samples

The scratching instrument outputs left and right sample values. For every active note, the synthesizer multiplies each channel by the XML dry and effect-send levels, then adds the results to the corresponding buses. Defaults come from the instrument tag; note attributes take precedence.

2. Process and mix

The effects processor advances its circular buffers, modulation phases, and feedback filters once per frame. It mixes the wet returns with dry audio, checks for non-finite values, and bounds the output to the expected normalized range. A 2.5-second tail allows delayed effects to decay after the last note.
